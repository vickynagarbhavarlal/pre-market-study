Global Overnight Cues

🔴 Wall Street • broad selloff

Thursday's US session ended sharply lower. Dow Jones fell 2.19% to 49,097.06, S&P 500 declined 2.02% to 7,175.83 and Nasdaq Composite lost 2.25% to 23,879.20. The S&P recorded its sixth consecutive losing session as technology and AI-linked shares came under renewed pressure.

🔴 Asia • semiconductor-led rout

At the morning snapshot, Japan's Nikkei 225 was down 4.72% and South Korea's Kospi fell 4.48%; Topix and Kosdaq were also sharply lower. Hong Kong futures pointed to a weak start. The regional selloff reflects spillover from US technology weakness and doubts around the AI investment cycle.

🔴 AI trade • valuation anxiety persists

Anthropic made a strong Nasdaq debut, opening at $111.50 against its $83 offer price and ending at $97.17, up 17.1%. Yet the listing failed to stabilise the wider technology trade. GIFT Nifty was around 25,361 at 07:07 IST, nearly 171 points below the previous futures close. This implies a weak start relative to Thursday's 25,514.30 cash close. Futures can change materially before 09:15 IST; use the live pre-open indication rather than treating this snapshot as a guaranteed opening level.

🟠 Crude • near $92

Brent crude was around $92.08 per barrel, up 0.11%, while WTI traded near $83.40, up 0.23%. Prices remain on track for a third weekly decline, although supply and shipping disruptions continue to create upside risk. A sustained fall in crude would help India's import bill.

🟠 Rates and dollar • defensive positioning

Safe-haven demand pushed Treasury yields lower overnight while the US dollar extended its advance. The market is also digesting a more hawkish Federal Reserve rate outlook. This combination can tighten financial conditions for emerging markets even when bond yields temporarily ease.

Swing-Trader Framework

1 • Do not buy the gap blindly

A large gap-down after a strong session can trigger both stop-loss selling and short covering. Let the first 15–30 minutes establish the opening range before taking a directional trade.

2 • Respect the pivot and prior low

Use Nifty 25,377.80 as the balance level, 25,241.30 as first support and 24,968.30 as the next downside reference. Thursday's low near 25,105 is the deeper structural test.

3 • Check sector leadership

Auto, consumer durables and PSU banks led Thursday's rally. Watch whether leaders hold relative strength; avoid assuming yesterday's top sector will automatically lead after a global shock.

4 • IT and semiconductor exposure

US technology weakness raises gap risk for IT and high-beta tech-linked names. Avoid averaging down solely because a stock has already fallen; require a base, volume confirmation and a defined invalidation level.

5 • Separate news from valuation

Coal India, NLC India, Paytm, ACME Solar and Dilip Buildcon have identifiable catalysts. Quantify the likely earnings and cash-flow impact instead of trading headlines without a valuation framework.

6 • Protect capital first

Keep risk per trade fixed, reduce exposure when volatility expands and avoid oversized overnight positions into uncertain global macro conditions. A missed trade costs nothing; a poorly sized gap risk can damage the account.

Strong domestic close, but global risk has taken control of the opening setup.

However, a 2%+ fall across major US indices, a sharp Asian technology selloff and GIFT Nifty near 25,361 create a meaningful gap-down risk. Avoid treating a gap fill as a confirmed reversal; demand breadth, VWAP and banking-sector confirmation. The preferred stance is selective, smaller-sized and capital-protective until the market demonstrates that Thursday's rebound can withstand the external shock.
